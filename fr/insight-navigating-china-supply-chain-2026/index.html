Navigating China's Supply Chain in 2026: A Buyer's Checklist

Publié le2026-06-15·6 min de lecture

Sourcing from China remains one of the most effective ways to access competitive manufacturing at scale — but the process rewards buyers who do their homework. The single biggest risk reduction comes from supplier verification: confirm business licenses, request factory audit reports, and where possible visit the production floor in person or via a trusted local partner before committing to volume orders.

Lead times vary significantly by product category and factory workload, particularly around Chinese New Year, when many factories close for two to four weeks. Buyers who plan orders with this seasonal calendar in mind avoid the scramble that catches first-time importers off guard.

Quality control should not be treated as a final checkpoint — it works best as a staged process: incoming raw material checks, in-process inspection during production, and a pre-shipment inspection before the goods leave the factory. Each stage catches different classes of defects, and catching them early is dramatically cheaper than catching them after landing.

Finally, payment terms and Incoterms should be negotiated explicitly and in writing. A common structure is a deposit against order confirmation with the balance due before shipment, but the right structure depends on your relationship history with the supplier and the value of the order.
