Factory Setup in East Africa: A Step-by-Step Guide

Ilichapishwa2026-05-02·8 dakika za kusoma

Establishing a factory in East Africa starts well before ground is broken. Site selection needs to weigh proximity to ports and labor markets against the cost and reliability of utilities — power stability in particular has an outsized effect on continuous-process manufacturing.

Regulatory pathways differ by country and sector: expect to navigate business registration, environmental impact assessments, and in many cases import-duty exemptions for productive equipment. Engaging local counsel and a customs specialist early prevents costly delays once machinery is already in transit.

Machinery sourcing and shipping is often the longest lead-time item in the whole project. Building a realistic project timeline means sequencing civil works, utility connections, and equipment delivery so that installation teams are not left waiting — or waiting to be paid — for delayed shipments.

Commissioning and operator training close the loop: a factory is only as good as the people running it day to day. Budgeting for a training period alongside the first production run, rather than treating it as an afterthought, has a direct effect on early yield rates.
